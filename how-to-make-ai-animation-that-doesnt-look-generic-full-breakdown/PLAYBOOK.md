# How To Make AI Animation That Doesn't Look Generic (Full Breakdown)

## Final Result
A dialogue scene from the animated short *Below the Surface*. Cave explorer Jennie sits in a drilling mecha deep underground and takes a video call from her sister Emily, who is in a hospital. Faces stay consistent across angles. Lips match real recorded voices. The result mixes AI generation with hand-painted finishes so it does not read as generic AI animation.

## Step-by-Step Recreation Playbook
1. Generate the Jennie face character sheet with the prompt above in SOUL 2.0.
2. Generate a second sheet for the outfit and red helmet version.
3. Use Seedream to combine face and outfit into one sheet.
4. Create the hair-only reference so the helmet can come off later.
5. Build the mecha two-view sheet and the cockpit reference video.
6. Record each dialogue line in ElevenLabs with bracketed delivery notes. Attach the best takes.
7. In Claude, turn the script into a shot plan, then into a full video prompt that maps every reference.
8. Generate the scene in short segments. Each segment gets its own prompt and the matching audio.
9. Hand-paint over the final stills and designs to remove generic AI texture.
10. Assemble the segments in the edit.

## Key Learnings & Replication Notes
Split dialogue into short generations. Long continuous prompts lose lip sync and acting.
Assign every reference one clear job. State what to take and what to ignore.
Hand paintover on character sheets and mecha removes the generic look.
Record voices first. Seedance then lip-syncs to real performances instead of inventing them.
A 360° video character reference locks face, hair, and clothing from every angle the camera might use.
Build recurring assets once, fix them by hand, and reuse them.
