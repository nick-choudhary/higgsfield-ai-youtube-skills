# Can AI Beat $10,000 VFX Artist?

## Final Result
A side-by-side comparison of a cinematic dinosaur chase sequence. One version used traditional VFX. The other used Higgsfield Cinema Studio, Seedance 2.5, Claude, and custom skills. The AI version places a feathered Velociraptor into forest footage, replaces a face, directs arrow flight, adds impact, improves run weight, stages a grenade fail, and ends with an explosion.

## Skill Installation
Download two Claude skill files from the skill page linked in the video description:

- higgsfield-dinosaur-anatomy.md
- higgsfield-dinosaur-motion.md

In Claude, open Skills settings. Upload both files. Activate them. Use anatomy when describing body structure. Use motion when describing how the dinosaur moves.

Also install the Higgsfield Instant Chrome extension from the second link in the description. It surfaces timestamped prompts and assets next to the YouTube player.

### Dinosaur First Version
Create a highly photorealistic natural-history reference sheet of one adult VELOCIRAPTOR MONGOLIENSIS, using this text alone, no reference images. A scientifically informed feathered reconstruction photographed as a living animal. Small, lightly built, low-bodied predator with a long narrow low skull, subtly upturned snout profile, slender neck, compact flesh-covered ribcage, well-proportioned thighs and a very long tapering bony tail held clear of ground. NOT the large bulky naked movie raptor. No beak: a narrow tooth-bearing reptilian snout. No ornamental crests or horns.

Landscape 3:2, plain warm light-gray studio with soft directional daylight, lifelike shadows, restrained photographic color, sharp detail. Design a spacious reference sheet: top half two large full-body views, exact left profile and front three-quarter view; lower half three panels, a smaller full-body slow walking pose, a large detailed head portrait with mouth slightly open, and a detailed complete foot close-up including the lower leg and all toes. All complete-body silhouettes fit with generous margins, including tail tip, feathers and feet. No text, fake labels or signatures. Same individual's coloration, head proportions and marking locations in every panel.

Feather covering: short layered contour feathers over body, neck and thighs, smoothly following fleshy volume rather than inflating into a huge bird ball; substantial long pennaceous feathers along the forearm and second finger forming folded wing-like arms; modest feathering along the long tail. Fine individual overlapping barbs and feather edges with soft irregular natural arrangement, not hair, fur or quills. Muted sandy-brown upper plumage with subtle darker barring, buff underside and gray-brown flight feathers. This color scheme and exact feather distribution are plausible artistic choices. Leave a restrained small area of fine-scaled skin around the snout and eye, and scaly lower feet. Do not expose a naked wrinkled turkey neck or bare muscular torso.

Three clawed fingers on each forelimb, palms facing each other, feathered arms gently folded against sides without anatomically fusing into chest. Show natural finger placement within the wing feathers, no extra hands. Digitigrade hindlimbs: elevated ankles, two forward supporting toes III and IV bearing weight on the ground, inner toe II held flexed UP with its distinctive curved sickle claw completely clear of the ground, small first toe above ground. The foot close-up clearly separates all digits. No giant fantasy claws. The walking panel shows one planted foot and the other lifted forward, with believable balance and small joint yielding.

Healthy flesh and soft tissues beneath plumage; subtle contour change with posture, no exposed muscle bundles or deep sculpted grooves. Fine facial skin with restrained shallow scale relief and slight eyelid folds; dry diffuse surface contrasting with wet convex cornea and moist mouth interior. Warm brown iris, round dark pupil, attentive natural gaze. Narrow jaws and slightly open mouth reveal individually varied small backward-curving laterally compressed ivory teeth embedded in gums, delicate serrations only at close range, no identical white triangular zipper or oversized T.rex teeth. Thin scaled oral margins, relaxed mouth, tongue rests inside, no snarling or roaring.

Make the animal feel alive through believable weight, subtle asymmetry, compliant exposed skin, alert gaze, soft feather overlap and realistic photographic material response. No toy sheen, resin, clay, plastic, glossy uniform scales, waxy skin, illustration, skeletal overlay, people or environment.

## Step-by-Step Recreation Playbook
1. Download the two dinosaur skill .md files from the skill page. Upload and activate them in Claude Skills.
2. Record or prepare a 360° head video of the target face under even light.
3. Prepare source footage. Blur the original actor's head where face replacement is needed.
4. Generate the dinosaur reference sheets using the first and final prompts above. Save the final version as an Element.
5. For each shot, attach the correct references (@shot_plate, @char_dinosaur, layout images, etc.). Paste the matching full prompt from the skill page into Cinema Studio with Seedance 2.5.
6. Use Claude with the motion skill active to refine movement descriptions when runs look stiff. Reference real animal gait (ostrich for head stabilization, cassowary for mass).
7. Generate, review, and iterate only the failing element (position, timing, weight).
8. Assemble the approved shots, match color, and add sound.

## Key Learnings & Replication Notes
Blur the original head before face replacement when identity mixes. Use a layout still to lock size and position before generating the full shot. Stretch short clips when timing needs more room. Describe physics and secondary motion (head delay, tail counter, weight settle) instead of just the action. Reuse a successful face fix across later shots. The motion skill encodes the tested run weight so later prompts stay consistent. Keep every plate pixel unchanged except the added creature and its shadow.
