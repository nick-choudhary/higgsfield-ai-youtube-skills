### Storyboard Prompt (Seedream 5.5, black-and-white sheets)
Use Higgsfield MCP and Seedream 5.5 to create eight separate black-and-white storyboard sheets for a funny WYT toothpaste commercial. Generate one sheet per call, in 16:9. Use clean pencil/ink drawings, simple grayscale shading, numbered panels and clear compositions. Keep the same character, room layout and props throughout.

STORY AND CONTINUITY

Mike is a young man with curly brown hair and an expressive smile. He starts barefoot in plain light-blue pajamas. His bathroom has a sink and mirror on the left and a washing machine on the right. WYT is a silver toothpaste tube with a white hinged flip-top cap; the cap stays attached, and the gel is transparent. After brushing, Mike notices his white teeth and tries the toothpaste on a towel, sneakers, the kitchen floor and his car. Later he wears a white suit and white sneakers, with platinum-blond hair. The gray and white cars are the same left-hand-drive car. A black cat becomes a white cat only in the final payoff.

SHEET 1 — MORNING ROUTINE

Shots 1–5: angled wide view of Mike at the bathroom sink; close-up of the tube in his left hand; close-up of gel going onto a toothbrush; overhead view of him brushing; low-angle view of the brushing.

SHEET 2 — THE DISCOVERY

Shots 7–9: Mike washes his face, smiles into the mirror, notices his teeth, then looks at the faded towel and compares their shades. Use the reflection for the reaction. Shot 6 is intentionally omitted; preserve these numbers.

SHEET 3 — THE EXPERIMENT

Shots 10–12: view from inside the washer drum as Mike loads the towel; close-up of gel going into the detergent drawer; close-up of his finger pressing START. He must not turn the program dial.

SHEET 4 — THE RESULT

Shots 13–14: Mike takes out the white towel without steam, compares it with his teeth in the mirror, then exits the frame. Keep Mike on the left and the washer on the right.

SHEET 5 — SHOES AND FLOOR

Shots 15–18: close-up of a toothbrush scrubbing a dirty sneaker; clean and dirty sneakers side by side; floor-level view of the brush; overhead kitchen shot showing a clear boundary between dirty and cleaned tiles.

SHEET 6 — A BIGGER IDEA

Shots 19–21: Mike leaves his house in the white suit with blond hair; overhead wide view showing the whole dull gray car; closer reaction as he rolls up his sleeves.

SHEET 7 — THE CAT

On the office street: close-up of the now-white car's wheel stopping; Mike exits the driver's door and closes it; black cat close-up; Mike's reaction as he says “No way”; locked wide shot of him chasing the cat to screen-right. Show the car transformation across a cut.

SHEET 8 — WYT AND THE PAYOFF

A clean product composition with the closed silver WYT tube and the readable lowercase slogan “make it bright”; then a frontal full-body wide shot of Mike sitting on a bench, calmly petting the white cat.

Keep every camera angle and action in this order. No color, extra characters, decorative captions or invented scenes. Return all eight sheets in sequence.

## Step-by-Step Recreation Playbook
1. Download the two .skill files from the skill page and upload them into Claude.
2. Connect Higgsfield MCP.
3. Explain the full story to Claude and instruct it to use the Seedance Prompt Writer skill to split the story into manageable video sections with start, action, and end states.
4. Generate eight black-and-white storyboard sheets using the storyboard prompt above and Seedream 5.5. Check angles and continuity before any video spend.
5. Create a base character sheet in Cinema Studio. Edit it with the character edit prompt to lock the pajama outfit while preserving identity and natural teeth.
6. Generate the bathroom location plate using the full location prompt. Test character + location together and save as Elements.
7. Prepare prop references: tube with open/closed states, towel in dull and bright-white versions. Use targeted image-to-image edits for slogan and color changes.
8. Generate video takes in Seedance 2.5. Attach the named reference images. Use the detailed bathroom prompt structure with timed shots and acting directions. Carry end states into the next shot.
9. Fix specific problems (glowing teeth, garbled text, wrong panel lettering) with small image-to-image repairs on single frames, then re-attach the corrected frame.
10. Repeat the asset and generation process for kitchen and outdoor scenes. Assemble selected takes into the final commercial.

## Key Learnings & Replication Notes
Generate storyboards and test asset pairs before spending video credits. Write hand actions in exact physical order. Use named @Image references and a style prefix so the model keeps lighting and camera consistent. For text or small defects, repair a single frame instead of regenerating the whole shot. Carry each shot’s end state into the next. The Acting Director skill keeps performance readable without spoken dialogue. Save working assets as Elements in Cinema Studio so later generations stay locked to the same character, room, and props.
