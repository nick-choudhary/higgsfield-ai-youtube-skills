### 1. Product Motion (PITAYA / dragon fruit)

```
Create an original dragon-fruit motion-design film from scratch. This brief contains the complete visual direction; no reference video or reference image is required. Generate the assets, create an editable Figma storyboard, and build the animation in After Effects. Show the storyboard for approval before animating. Deliver a clean 1920 × 1080, 30 fps film of approximately 20–21 seconds, plus the editable project and its linked assets.

VISUAL LANGUAGE
Use hot pink #FF2794, lime #BDEA36, warm cream #F4F0E6, and near-black #101411. Alternate pink and lime scenes with calm dark and cream scenes. Use SCHABO for tall, tightly condensed English headlines and a small monospace font only for useful specimen labels. If SCHABO is unavailable, use a similarly narrow condensed display font while preserving the tall proportions. Combine photorealistic fruit with flat geometric graphics, thin construction grids, moving crop windows, and restrained tracking marks. Keep the composition spacious and the camera stable during holds.

ORIGINAL ASSETS
Generate a coherent set of dragon-fruit images in Higgsfield: a small green bud, young pink fruit, developing fruit with green scales opening outward, ripe whole fruit, and a cut half with white flesh and black seeds. Treat this as a stylized growth progression. Keep fruit proportions, lighting, pink skin, and green tips consistent. Seeds belong inside the exposed flesh. Also generate macro details of wet pink skin, green tips, white flesh, and seeds. Use clean cutouts with genuine alpha transparency and sufficient resolution for close-ups. Generate short clips of droplets moving down the whole fruit and the cut half; trim each to about three seconds. Keep the fruit shape stable while droplets merge and slide, with subtle highlight movement. Angle the hero fruit approximately 15 degrees for a dynamic pose.

CORE SEQUENCE
Build the following fast sequence once, then develop three musical passes using different fruit views and pink/lime variations. These timings describe an approximately 6.7-second design sketch; adapt the final timing to the soundtrack rather than repeating an arbitrary duration.

1. 0.00–0.80: Begin on warm cream with a tiny centered fruit and a minimal construction grid. Grow it smoothly into a larger specimen, using controlled acceleration and a clean stop. Keep the opening frame simple and centered.

2. 0.80–2.35: Reveal five cropped cards across the frame, labeled Bud, Young, Opening, Ripe, and Cut. Stagger their entrances, scales, vertical positions, and slight rotations. Send a smooth wave through the row, then reverse the wave. Use near-black card backgrounds and alternate lime and pink behind the row. Keep the five stages readable.

3. 2.35–3.50: Transition the cards into separate SCHABO letters spelling INSIDE OUT. Let the letters arrive along a gently rising wave, then settle into a clear title. Use pink letters on near-black. Each letter must remain independently editable and animated.

4. 3.50–4.83: Cut into extreme fruit textures. Reveal four independently moving crop windows containing skin, flesh, seeds, and green tips. Connect selected crop corners with thin lines and tiny square markers. Use small Surface_01–04 labels. Add brief photographic inversions, then replace the large fruit image with a coarse, flat pixel silhouette that expands into the next background.

5. 4.83–6.70: Reveal the tilted hero fruit with moving droplets on near-black. Place large LIVING and TEXTURE text at the upper-left and lower-left, moving at slightly different phases behind the fruit. Introduce the cut half and a brief inverted detail. Finish with a transition that flows into the next musical pass; on the final pass, resolve into a clean hero composition.

MOTION TRACKING
Make tracking a visible part of the design. Attach small square markers to selected actual moving features: letter terminals, notches, corners, and crop edges. Connect related moving markers with thin lines. Compute their positions from the animated layer transforms so they remain attached during translation, rotation, and scaling. Reveal markers as movement begins and fade them out as the feature settles. Use short local connections and preserve letter readability. Avoid enclosing each letter in a full rectangular box or covering the scene with a dense network.

TIMING AND SOUND
Use the supplied soundtrack as the master clock. Measure its beats and musical phrases before finalizing the edit. Align major cuts, color changes, letter impacts, inversions, and repeat boundaries to actual sound accents. Adjust individual scene lengths while preserving the narrative order and smooth movement. If no soundtrack is supplied, work against a 140 BPM guide and export a silent preview: four bars last approximately 6.857 seconds, and three passes last approximately 20.571 seconds. Place boundaries on the nearest video frames using absolute musical time, so rounding does not accumulate across repeats.

EXECUTION AND DELIVERY
Use eased keyframes, staggered timing, subtle controlled overshoot, and natural motion blur on fast movement. Keep static shots completely still and retain crisp text during holds. Preserve native text, separate images, shape layers, meaningful layer names, precompositions, and editable controls. Check the real render for clipping, detached tracking marks, unreadable overlaps, flickering edges, missing footage, and beat alignment. Deliver the clean film without watermarks, interface panels, logos, or a fake timeline. Include the collected After Effects project, generated assets, and Figma storyboard.
```

### 2. ASCII and Number Animation

Attach the black-and-white source clip.

```
Use my attached black-and-white video as the source for an After Effects ASCII effect. Preserve its aspect ratio, selected time range, frame rate and original motion.

Build a small JSX control panel with a Python frame-conversion stage. Let me select the video, choose the in/out range, and set rows and columns. Keep the grid fixed; fewer rows and columns should produce larger characters. Use mixed Inter weights and strong contrast. Map brightness to character coverage so dense characters describe highlights and lighter marks describe darker areas. Process both foreground and background, including black areas.

Keep static regions stable and suppress unnecessary flicker. Do not add scattered particles, falling characters or falloff. Keep each character in a fixed cell so glyph changes do not shift the grid.

Cache the conversion and output two transparent PNG sequences: the main image and the background symbols. Import them as separate footage layers in After Effects, synchronized with the source. Keep colours and opacity adjustable in AE, with the transparency available for compositing over another background. Preserve a source layer for matching-frame comparisons.

Support replacing the footage and rebuilding its cache. Make clear which settings require a new conversion. Review the image at normal speed for recognizable silhouettes, stable static areas, correct duration and consistent character alignment.

Deliver the JSX panel, Python converter and required setup instructions, cached PNG sequences, and an editable AE project. The character sequences are raster images; do not represent them as separately editable text glyphs.
```

Finished brief:

```
Build a polished 14.52-second vertical motion edit from the attached Clipmakers-Before.mp4 in After Effects. Use its original voice and performance. The source is 1080 × 1920, 25 fps, 363 frames. Keep that duration and frame rate. I want purposeful zooms, dynamic captions synchronized to individual spoken words, and conceptual cutaways created with Higgsfield. Follow this written direction without an external style reference.

SOURCE AND SETUP

Create a new AE project and import the attached Before video, preserving its voice, timing, and original file. Crop the baked-in platform interface using x=50, y=0, width=864, height=1536, then scale uniformly to fill the portrait composition. Check the speaker’s head, hands, and framing throughout. Keep the project organized and editable.

TIMING AND WORDS

Keep the dialogue unchanged: no rewritten lines, invented words, synthesized voice, or retiming of the performance. Start with this transcript and verify every word against the source audio before placing captions. These are approximate speech ranges, not frame-accurate caption cues:

1. 1.12–3.02: “Back then I had no idea what to do.”
2. 3.30–5.34: “It felt like the ground disappeared from under my feet.”
3. 5.98–7.54: “But that’s exactly when I realized”
4. 8.14–9.60: “no one is coming to save you.”
5. 10.04–11.88: “Either you get up or you stay down.”
6. 12.26–14.26: “I got up and that changed everything.”

Use the waveform and playback to refine word onsets on the 25 fps timeline. Captions should reveal on the spoken beat, remain readable, and clear before the next phrase competes with them. Preserve natural pauses.

VISUAL SYSTEM

Use electric lime, approximately #D2ED49, near-black silhouettes, and warm white type. Build a deliberate contrast between the warm, photographic talking head and stark graphic cutaways.

Combine a heavy contemporary sans-serif for connective words with a large expressive italic serif for selected emotional words. Choose installed fonts and record their names. Avoid covering the eyes or mouth. Keep important type within safe margins.

Use short scale and position accents, restrained overshoot, directional blur, and occasional pixelated type entrances. Effects must resolve to sharp, readable words. Use a lime underline selectively, not under every caption.

SCENE PLAN

0.00–3.40: Establish the speaker. Introduce “Back then” in bold white, a smaller “I had,” and a prominent lime italic “no idea.” Resolve the next phrase into a stacked “WHAT / TO DO” composition with lime emphasis. Animate each part to speech rather than displaying the entire paragraph immediately. Keep the opening framing calm before the first strong transition.

3.40–6.08: Replace the picture with a lime field and a black human silhouette. Begin with a grounded walking or standing pose. As the speech describes the ground disappearing, make the silhouette lose support, tilt, drop, and shrink into depth. Let “under” rotate above it and “my feet” curve or angle around the falling motion. Keep these words as editable text. End with clear negative space so the return to the speaker feels intentional.

6.08–8.20: Return to a closer speaker crop. Emphasize “exactly” in italic white and make “realized” the largest word, with a brief pixelated entrance and a lime underline. Let the zoom settle; avoid continuously drifting the camera.

8.20–10.16: Show a seated or low, reaching black figure against lime while larger passing silhouettes move through the foreground. Their indifference illustrates “no one is coming to save you.” Keep “no one…” prominent in italic serif and the remaining words compact and readable. Preserve separate foreground and background layers for depth and timing control.

10.16–12.12: Cut to a high-angle figure on hands and knees beginning to push upward. Add a soft dark vignette and contact shadow. Place “GET UP” above the figure and “OR YOU STAY DOWN” below, appearing with the words. Use the figure’s motion to support the meaning rather than adding unrelated spectacle.

12.12–14.52: Return to the speaker. Build “I got” in bold type and a large italic “up,” then resolve “and that changed everything” into a final readable emphasis. End on the original duration without a new CTA or logo.

ASSETS, BUILD, AND CHECK

Use Higgsfield for the minimum necessary silhouette artwork or short figure motion. Request plain backgrounds, consistent proportions, clean edges, no typography, no logos, and no interface. Import the generated files as separate assets. Animate layout, typography, masks, shadows, zooms, and transitions inside AE. Keep figure footage separate from the editable type and graphics.

Organize SOURCE, AUDIO, HIGGSFIELD_ASSETS, CAPTIONS, CUTAWAYS, and MASTER. Use native text, shape layers, precomps, null controls, clear layer names, and editable effects. Keep source audio on its own layer, with any added sound effects separate and quiet enough for clear speech.

Preview the complete edit, check every word and transition, and save the project with collected assets. Give brief progress updates and leave the master composition open.
```

## Key Learnings & Replication Notes

- Three tools, three jobs: Higgsfield makes pixels, Astra runs the brief, After Effects holds the editable project.
- Motion skills live in the ChatGPT plugin, not as a downloadable `.skill` in this episode.
- `@higgsfield install /after-effects` is the video setup line. `/use-after-effects` is the working command.
- Product Motion wants a storyboard gate. Do not jump to the 21-second film.
- ASCII output is raster sequences. Do not pretend the glyphs are live text.
- Talking-head crop is published: x=50 y=0 w=864 h=1536 on a 1080×1920 25 fps source.
- Illustration path redraws shapes. Moving a bitmap cutout is a failed take.
- Localization and 9:16 both require the AEP. An exported video is not enough.
- Article timing for the unused-soundtrack Product Motion fallback: 140 BPM, four bars ≈ 6.857s, three passes ≈ 20.571s.
- Plugin generations spend credits.
