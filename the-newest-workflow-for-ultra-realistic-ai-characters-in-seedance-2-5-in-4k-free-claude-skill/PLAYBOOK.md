# The Newest Workflow For Ultra Realistic AI Characters in Seedance 2.5 in 4K (Free Claude Skill)

## Final Result
Realistic AI creatures that look like practical-effects makeup tests from a real film set, not video-game characters. The pipeline produces grey-clay sculptures, real-animal face references, macro material swatches, and a boring flash-photo composite, then animates the result in Seedance 2.5 at native 4K. The same method works for props and (with a change) for locations.

## Skill Installation
1. Connect Higgsfield MCP in Claude.
   - Open Claude (web or desktop).
   - Go to Customize → Connectors (or Settings → Connectors).
   - Click + → Add custom connector.
   - Name it Higgsfield.
   - Paste the URL: https://mcp.higgsfield.ai/mcp
   - Click Add / Connect and sign in with your Higgsfield account.
2. Download the free skills from the video page and add them to the Claude chat:
   - Higgsfield-Creature-Sculpt-Pipeline.skill — describe a creature in one sentence; it runs the four steps (clay, face, materials, final photo).
   - Higgsfield-Locations.skill — builds rooms and locations in Soul Cinema.
3. In a new chat, turn the Higgsfield connector on via the + menu → Connectors.
4. Talk to Claude in plain language. It writes the real prompts and calls the models.

### Seedance 2.5 4K underwater sequence (25 s, three shots)
Create a 25-second photoreal live-action underwater sequence in 21:9, three shots with hard cuts at 6 and 13 seconds.

Use @Image 1 for the alien. Do not take its wall, floor, flash lighting, hard shadow or pose. From @Image 2 take only the exit door with the lamp above it. Isolate the door from everything else in that image: no corridor, walls, floor, ceiling lights, windows or handrails. Ignore the door's size and framing in @Image 2: in this scene it is always a distant object. The door stands alone in the water and stays closed.

Setting: a sealed tank of almost black water whose walls are never seen. A dark underwater murk swallows everything distant, so things emerge only as the camera comes close; the murk is never grey or milky. Very fine, sparse particles hang perfectly still at different depths and shift only by parallax as the camera moves. The black carries a faint dark green tint. No bubbles, no glow, no film grain.

Light: a faint, constant ambient glow from far above, just enough to separate the alien from the black. It never fades up or switches on and is never a spotlight: no pool of light, no bright rim, no visible beam. The only light that switches on is the lamp above the far-off door.

Lens: full-frame anamorphic at 35mm, 1.8x squeeze, T2.3, with an expressive anamorphic character: shallow depth of field, tall oval bokeh, natural proportions. The frame is evenly bright to every corner: no vignetting, no dark corners, no black oval edges, no blurred border.

SHOT 1 — 0–6 s
A very wide, strictly frontal opening shot. Almost the whole frame is empty black water. The alien is far away, about thirty metres from the lens, centred and facing the camera: a small figure, about one-eighth of the frame height, complete from crest to toes, with vast dark water above, below and on both sides.

0–6 s: the camera glides forward very slowly and smoothly and covers only a short distance, so he barely grows and is still no more than one-sixth of the frame height at the cut. Still particles slide past the lens at different depths and give the feeling of travelling forward through deep water. Out of the murk he gradually emerges: first a barely visible upright shape, then crest, shoulders and limbs.

He floats weightless and asleep, the way a body drifts in still water, never as if standing: there is no floor under him. The pose is loose and uneven. One leg trails slightly behind the other with its knee a little bent, so the feet hang at different heights with the toes pointing down. The shoulders are dropped, one a touch lower than the other. The arms hang slack a little away from the torso, not mirrored, fingers softly curled. The head is bowed, eyes closed. He does not move.

SHOT 2 — 6–13 s
Hard cut to a centred frontal medium shot of his head and ribcage, the camera still drifting forward at the same slow speed, with room above the crest.

6–10 s: he sleeps, completely still. The slow approach builds tension.

10–11 s: his head snaps upright and his eyes open in one violent instant. Only the head moves; the torso stays still. A faint wet glint in each eye.

11–12 s: he comes to. He holds, blinks once, and his gaze moves across the dark space: not fear, but confusion, a mind that does not know where it is or what it is.

12–13 s: his gaze drops to his own body. He begins to raise his hands.

SHOT 3 — 13–25 s — ONE CONTINUOUS TAKE, NO CUTS
Hard cut to a close low-angle three-quarter shot on the wide lens, with a light, smooth handheld feel and a level horizon. The camera is below his chest and off to camera-left, on his right side, so his right hand is nearer the lens than his left. Framed from mid-thigh to above the crest.

13–16.5 s: he raises both hands in front of his chest and slowly turns them, palms up, then palms down, fingers uncurling: he is seeing his own body for the first time. Meanwhile the camera drifts a short distance sideways, slowly and smoothly. Focus starts on his face, racks boldly onto the near right hand so the face melts into blur, then racks back to his face.

16.5–17.5 s: the moment focus returns to his face, the lamp above the door switches on far behind the camera. Its soft reflex lands on his face and upper chest and stays steady. He reacts: his hands stop, he lifts his head and looks forward, just past the camera, toward the light.

17.5–19.5 s: from the same spot the camera pans right about 165 degrees, away from him, the image softening during the move. The camera only turns: it does not travel toward the door and it does not zoom.

19.5–20.5 s: hold on the far end of the tank. Almost the whole frame is empty black water. Deep in the distance, about a hundred metres away, the door is a tiny speck: what reads first is its lamp, one small point of warm light, with the door only just discernible beneath it. Door and lamp together take up less than one-twentieth of the frame height and sit near the centre, with vast dark water above, below and on both sides. The murk dims them and keeps their edges soft. No close view of the door at any moment.

20.5–22 s: the camera pans back left along the same path and refocuses on his face in the same low-angle three-quarter framing. He has not moved.

22–23 s: he stays in place for one second and lowers his hands all the way to his sides.

23–25 s: he moves forward toward the door. His whole body tips a few degrees forward and glides as one unit, without swimming strokes or kicks, grows in the frame, passes the camera on its right and leaves the frame completely by 25 s. The camera stays where it is and does not follow.

## Step-by-Step Recreation Playbook
1. Connect Higgsfield MCP as described above. Confirm with a credit-balance query.
2. Add the Creature Sculpt Pipeline skill to the chat.
3. Describe the creature in one sentence (example: "a demon king: half bear, half wolf, with boar tusks, wearing his trophies").
4. Let the skill generate:
   - Grey clay bust (Soul 2 or Seedream 5.5). Iterate until the shape is correct.
   - Real-animal face reference (Soul 2).
   - Macro material swatches for every distinct surface (Soul 2).
   - Final boring flash-photo composite (Seedream 5.5) that locks shape, face and materials.
5. For animation: feed the final photo into Seedance 2.5. Draft at 480p first (3 credits/s), pick the best take, then finalize in 4K.
6. For locations: use the Locations skill or a direct Soul Cinema prompt. Skip clay for rooms.
7. For props: run the same clay → materials → flash-photo path.

## Key Learnings & Replication Notes
- One-prompt creatures produce plastic skin or mushy texture that falls apart under motion.
- Separate shape (clay), face (real animal photo), and materials (macro swatches). Then composite in a boring flash photo on a grey backdrop.
- Soul 2 is strong for faces and materials. Seedream 5.5 is better for complex anatomy and the final composite. Seedance 2.5 4K holds the textures in motion.
- Draft in 480p, pick the take, then upscale to 4K. Four 8-second drafts cost far less than four 4K generations.
- Rooms and locations: skip clay. Soul Cinema alone produces more believable, imperfect spaces.
- The same pipeline works for props (hammers, masks, furniture) and for creatures.
- The free skill collapses the four steps into one Claude conversation.
